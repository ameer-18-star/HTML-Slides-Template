Wired vs Wireless

Wired connections (USB, proprietary chargers) offer the fastest, most reliable data transfer and charging.

Wireless connections (Bluetooth, NFC, Wi-Fi) trade some speed and reliability for convenience and range.

1.1.1  ·  06

USB Standards

USB is the dominant standard for both charging and data transfer on modern mobile devices.

Connector shape (Type-A, Type-C) is independent of the underlying USB version and its data speed.

Key Points
USB-C is reversible and supports higher power delivery (up to 240W with USB PD 3.1)
USB4 runs over a USB-C connector and can reach 40 Gbps
USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 reaches 20 Gbps over USB-C
Connector shape does not guarantee a specific USB version

Procedure  ·  08

Mobile Battery Replacement

01
Power Off the Device Completely
Never work on a battery while the device is powered on or charging.
02
Remove the Back Cover
Use a plastic pry tool or suction cup — never a metal screwdriver. Heat may be needed on glued assemblies.
03
Disconnect the Battery Connector
Lift the ZIF connector tab straight up. Never pull by the cable ribbon — always disconnect from the socket end.
04
Remove and Replace the Battery
Use a plastic spudger to lift the battery. Insert the replacement, reconnect the connector, and press firmly to seat it.
05
Reassemble and Test
Reattach the back cover, power on the device, and verify the battery charges and reports the correct capacity.

Mobile Accessories  ·  09

Common Accessories — Click to Expand

Headsets & Earbuds
Wired (3.5 mm / USB-C) and wireless (Bluetooth) audio accessories
Wired headsets connect via the 3.5 mm audio jack or USB-C. Wireless earbuds use Bluetooth pairing. Exam tip: USB-C headsets require no audio jack and can carry digital audio — the device handles D/A conversion, not the headset.
expand
Speakers
Portable speakers connect via Bluetooth or 3.5 mm audio jack
Bluetooth speakers pair once and reconnect automatically. 3.5 mm speakers require no pairing but are limited by cable length. Exam tip: Bluetooth uses the ISM 2.4 GHz band and can experience interference from Wi-Fi on the same frequency.
expand
Game Controllers
Bluetooth or USB-C controllers for mobile gaming on phones and tablets
Mobile game controllers clip around the phone or connect via Bluetooth. Some connect through USB-C for zero-latency input. Exam tip: these are standard HID (Human Interface Device) class devices — no special driver is needed on modern mobile OSes.
expand
Drawing Pads
Stylus-based input pads for digital art and note-taking on tablets
Drawing pads use electromagnetic resonance (EMR) or capacitive stylus technology. EMR styli require no battery — the pad sends power to the pen. Active stylus types (like Apple Pencil) do require charging. Exam tip: EMR pads do not respond to finger touch — stylus only.

Concept  ·  10

What Is eSIM?

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a carrier profile provisioned directly onto a chip soldered to the device's motherboard, rather than a removable physical card.

Because there's no physical card to swap, switching carriers is done entirely through software — often by scanning a QR code the carrier provides.

Exam Takeaways
eSIM requires no physical card slot
Carrier profiles are provisioned over the network
Many modern phones support dual-SIM via one physical SIM plus one eSIM
eSIM cannot be physically removed if you switch devices

Hardware Components  ·  11

SIM Card Types

A SIM (Subscriber Identity Module) card stores the credentials that identify a phone to the cellular network. The physical size of the SIM has shrunk over generations while its function has remained unchanged.

Standard SIM, microSIM, and nanoSIM all carry the same gold contact chip — only the plastic frame around it differs. eSIM is embedded directly onto the motherboard, requiring no physical card at all.

Compare  ·  13

Standard SIM vs eSIM

Physical SIM
  • Removable — can be moved between devices instantly
  • No carrier software setup required
  • Vulnerable to being lost or physically damaged
  • Requires a SIM ejector tool to remove
eSIM
  • Cannot be physically removed or swapped
  • Activated via QR code or carrier app
  • Immune to physical card damage or loss
  • Not yet supported on every carrier or device

Mobile Storage Options

Internal (eMMC)
  • Fixed, non-removable storage
  • Lower cost, slower speeds
  • Common in budget devices
Internal (UFS / NVMe)
  • Fixed, non-removable storage
  • High-speed, found in flagship devices
  • Cannot be expanded after purchase
MicroSD
  • Removable, expandable storage
  • Speed varies widely by card class
  • Being phased out on many flagship phones

Safety  ·  16

Exam Trap
Never Use a Metal Tool Near a Battery

Puncturing or shorting a lithium-ion battery with a metal screwdriver or spudger can cause it to swell, overheat, or ignite. Always use plastic prying tools when opening a mobile device, and immediately stop work if a battery appears swollen.

Wireless  ·  18

Wireless Connection Methods

Mobile devices use several short-range wireless standards, each suited to a different use case. Click a tab to explore each one.

Method 01
Bluetooth

Bluetooth is a short-range wireless standard operating in the 2.4 GHz ISM band, used for headsets, keyboards, speakers, and file transfer.

  • Typical range: 10 meters (Class 2)
  • Requires a one-time pairing process
  • Can experience interference from Wi-Fi on the same band

Method 02
Near Field Communication

NFC operates at a very short range — typically under 4 cm — and is used for contactless payments and quick pairing.

  • No manual pairing required — just tap
  • Used for Apple Pay, Google Pay, and transit cards
  • Much lower data rate than Bluetooth

Method 03
Wi-Fi Direct

Wi-Fi Direct lets two devices connect peer-to-peer using Wi-Fi hardware, without a router, at much higher speeds than Bluetooth.

  • Used for screen mirroring and fast file transfer
  • Higher power draw than Bluetooth

LCD vs OLED — Click to Compare

LCD Display
LCD Display flip
Display Type 01
LCD

Liquid Crystal Display uses a constant backlight behind a liquid crystal layer. Cannot produce true black — used widely in budget and mid-range devices.

  • Backlight always on — dark grey, not true black
  • IPS panels offer wide viewing angles and accurate colour
  • More durable — less susceptible to burn-in than OLED
  • Thicker — requires space for the backlight layer

OLED Display
OLED Display flip
Display Type 02
OLED

Organic Light-Emitting Diode panels light each pixel individually — no backlight needed. Produces true blacks and higher contrast than LCD.

  • Each pixel emits its own light — true blacks
  • Thinner and more power-efficient on dark content
  • Susceptible to burn-in with static images over time
  • Standard on flagship phones and premium tablets
