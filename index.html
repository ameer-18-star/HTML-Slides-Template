Wired Connections  ·  06

Universal Serial Bus (USB)

USB is the most common wired connection standard for mobile devices. It carries both power and data over a single cable, making it the go-to choice for charging and file transfer between phones, tablets, and computers.

USB-C is now the dominant connector — reversible, fast, and capable of carrying DisplayPort and Thunderbolt signals. Older devices use microUSB or the proprietary Apple Lightning port, both of which the exam expects you to identify by sight.

USB standards differ in transfer speed: USB 2.0 peaks at 480 Mbps, USB 3.0 at 5 Gbps, and USB 3.2 Gen 2 at 10 Gbps. Speed and connector type are separate — a USB-C port can be USB 2.0 speed.

Key Points
USB-C is reversible and supports power delivery up to 100 W
microUSB was the Android standard before USB-C replaced it
Lightning is Apple-only — not a USB standard despite the shape
Connector shape does not determine USB speed — check the spec

Wireless Standards  ·  07

Wireless Connection Methods

Bluetooth
Short-range wireless for headsets, speakers, and keyboards. Pairs devices within ~10 metres with no internet required.
NFC
Near Field Communication — works within 4 cm. Used for tap-to-pay, file sharing, and pairing Bluetooth devices instantly.
Infrared (IR)
Line-of-sight only, short range. Older standard now mainly used as a TV remote emulator built into some phones.
Wi-Fi
IEEE 802.11 standard for wireless networking. Mobile devices connect to access points for internet and local network access.
Hotspot Tethering
Shares a phone's cellular data connection with other devices over Wi-Fi, USB, or Bluetooth tethering.
GPS
Receives signals from satellites to determine location. Receive-only — the device does not transmit to the satellite network.
Cellular (4G / 5G)
Mobile broadband via carrier towers. 4G LTE and 5G provide high-speed internet without a Wi-Fi access point.
Thunderbolt
High-speed wired interface combining PCIe and DisplayPort. Up to 40 Gbps on Thunderbolt 4. Uses USB-C connector on modern devices.

Step-by-Step  ·  08

Battery Replacement Procedure

01
Power Off the Device
Hold the power button and shut down completely. Never replace a battery while the device is on.
02
Apply ESD Precautions
Put on an anti-static wrist strap and work on an ESD mat. Ground yourself before touching any internal component.
03
Remove the Back Cover
Use a plastic pry tool or suction cup — never a metal screwdriver. Heat may be needed on glued assemblies.
04
Disconnect the Battery Connector
Lift the ZIF connector tab straight up. Never pull by the cable ribbon — always disconnect from the socket end.
05
Remove and Replace the Battery
Use a plastic spudger to lift the battery. Insert the replacement, reconnect the connector, and press firmly to seat it.
06
Reassemble and Test
Reattach the back cover, power on the device, and verify the battery charges and reports the correct capacity.

Mobile Accessories  ·  09

Common Accessories — Click to Expand

Headsets & Earbuds
Wired (3.5 mm / USB-C) and wireless (Bluetooth) audio accessories
Wired headsets connect via the 3.5 mm audio jack or USB-C. Wireless earbuds use Bluetooth pairing. Exam tip: USB-C headsets require no audio jack and can carry digital audio — the device handles D/A conversion, not the headset.
expand
Speakers
Portable speakers connect via Bluetooth or 3.5 mm audio jack
Bluetooth speakers pair once and reconnect automatically. 3.5 mm speakers require no pairing but are limited by cable length. Exam tip: Bluetooth uses the ISM 2.4 GHz band and can experience interference from Wi-Fi on the same frequency.
expand
Game Controllers
Bluetooth or USB-C controllers for mobile gaming on phones and tablets
Mobile game controllers clip around the phone or connect via Bluetooth. Some connect through USB-C for zero-latency input. Exam tip: these are standard HID (Human Interface Device) class devices — no special driver is needed on modern mobile OSes.
expand
Drawing Pads
Stylus-based input pads for digital art and note-taking on tablets
Drawing pads use electromagnetic resonance (EMR) or capacitive stylus technology. EMR styli require no battery — the pad sends power to the pen. Active stylus types (like Apple Pencil) do require charging. Exam tip: EMR pads do not respond to finger touch — stylus only.
expand
Protective Covers & Cases
Impact protection, screen protectors, and water-resistant cases
Cases reduce damage from drops. Screen protectors are tempered glass or plastic film. Waterproof cases add an IP-rated seal. Exam tip: IP68 means the device is dust-tight and can survive submersion beyond 1 metre — know the IP rating scale for the exam.
expand
Credit Card Readers
Square-style dongles for mobile point-of-sale via 3.5 mm or Bluetooth
Mobile card readers plug into the 3.5 mm jack or connect via Bluetooth. They convert card swipe/chip data into audio signals or encrypted Bluetooth packets sent to a POS app. Exam tip: these are not standard USB HID devices — they communicate through the audio port or BT stack.

Display Technology  ·  10

LCD vs OLED Displays

LCD (Liquid Crystal Display) panels use a backlight behind a liquid crystal layer to produce images. The backlight is always on, so true black is impossible — the crystals simply block most of the light, producing very dark grey at best.

OLED (Organic Light-Emitting Diode) displays work differently: each pixel generates its own light. When a pixel is black it is completely off, producing perfect black and enabling infinite contrast ratios. This also means OLED screens can be thinner and more power-efficient when displaying dark content.

For the exam, know that LCD requires a backlight and cannot achieve true black, while OLED pixels self-illuminate. AMOLED (Active Matrix OLED) is the version used in most Android flagship phones — it adds a thin-film transistor layer for faster pixel switching.

Exam Takeaways
LCD uses a backlight — OLED pixels self-illuminate
OLED achieves true black; LCD cannot
AMOLED is OLED with an active matrix layer for speed
IPS LCD offers better colour accuracy than TN panels
Digitiser + display are often one fused assembly in phones

Hardware Components  ·  11

SIM Card Types

A SIM (Subscriber Identity Module) card stores the credentials that identify a phone to the cellular network. The physical size of the SIM has shrunk over generations while its function has remained unchanged.

Standard SIM, microSIM, and nanoSIM all carry the same gold contact chip — only the plastic frame around it differs. eSIM is embedded directly onto the motherboard, requiring no physical card at all. The carrier profile is provisioned over the network instead.

Compare & Contrast  ·  13

USB-C vs Lightning

USB-C
  • Universal — used across Android, laptops, tablets, and accessories
  • Reversible — symmetrical connector inserts either way up
  • Power Delivery — supports up to 240 W on USB PD 3.1
  • Multi-protocol — carries USB, Thunderbolt, DisplayPort, and HDMI
Lightning (Apple)
  • Proprietary — Apple-only, not compatible with USB standard
  • Speed Limited — maxes out at USB 2.0 speeds (480 Mbps)
  • Being Phased Out — Apple moving to USB-C on newer iPhones
  • Adapter Required — needs a Lightning-to-USB-A or USB-C adapter

USB Speed Standards

  • 480 Mbps max transfer speed
  • Common on older phones and accessories
  • Lightning ports limited to this speed

  • 5–10 Gbps transfer speed
  • Blue port colour on USB-A connectors
  • Most current Android flagship standard

  • USB-C connector only — no USB-A

Exam Critical  ·  16

Exam Trap
A USB-C port does not guarantee USB 3.x speed

USB-C is a connector shape — not a speed specification. A port with a USB-C connector can be USB 2.0, USB 3.2, or Thunderbolt 4. Always check the device spec sheet. The exam will test whether you confuse the physical connector with the underlying USB standard — these are independent of each other.

Internal Components

Component 01
Battery

Lithium-ion (Li-ion) and lithium-polymer (LiPo) batteries power modern mobile devices. LiPo batteries are thinner and can be shaped to fit irregular spaces inside slim devices.

  • Capacity measured in milliamp-hours (mAh)
  • ESD precautions required during replacement
  • Swollen batteries are a safety hazard — replace immediately

Component 02
Display

The display assembly in a smartphone combines the LCD or OLED panel with a digitiser (touch sensor) fused together. Replacing one usually means replacing both as a single unit.

  • LCD — requires backlight, cannot achieve true black
  • OLED — self-illuminating pixels, true black output
  • Digitiser — the touch layer fused on top of the panel

Component 03
Camera

Modern smartphones have multiple rear cameras and at least one front-facing camera. Camera modules connect to the motherboard via ZIF (Zero Insertion Force) ribbon connectors.

  • ZIF connector — lift the locking tab before removing
  • OIS — Optical Image Stabilisation built into lens module
  • Calibration may be required after camera replacement
Add camera module image here
Component 04
Memory

Mobile devices use two types of memory: RAM for active processes and NAND flash storage for the OS and user data. Unlike desktop RAM, mobile RAM is soldered to the motherboard and cannot be upgraded.

  • RAM — soldered on, cannot be replaced or upgraded
  • microSD — removable flash storage on supported devices
  • UFS / eMMC — internal NAND storage soldered to board
