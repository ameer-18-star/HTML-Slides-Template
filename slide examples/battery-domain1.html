Hardware & Device Replacement  ·  09 / 30

Repair It, or Replace It?

Damaged hardware either gets repaired, or replaced entirely — some components simply aren't worth fixing once they fault.

Repairable
  • RAM · Storage drives · Battery
  • Keyboard · Display · Power jack
  • Speakers · Trackpad
  • ...if they show a single, isolated fault.
Non-Repairable
  • BGA chips (soldered to motherboard)
  • Faulty PCB traces · Damaged ICs
  • Liquid-damaged components
  • Soldered or damaged CPU / GPU

Repair vs. Replace — Defined

Repair

Fixing a specific fault and restoring the component to its original functionality.

  • Replacing a faulty capacitor on the motherboard
  • Fixing a loose connection or wire
  • Cleaning or replacing a faulty fan
Replace

Swapping a faulty or damaged component with a new one — beyond repair, or not worth fixing.

  • Swapping out a faulty hard drive
  • Replacing a damaged display panel
  • Upgrading to a new processor or RAM

How to Decide

Three practical questions settle almost every repair-or-replace call.

Cost

Time

Practicality

Is the repair feasible at all, or would replacement be more reliable?

What Is a Battery?

Definition

An electrochemical device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy — providing power when external AC power is unavailable, and enabling portability.

Laptop batteries ship with AC adapters for recharging, but the device runs primarily on its internal battery whenever it's unplugged.

Comparing Battery Chemistries

Three metrics decide how a battery chemistry stacks up.

Energy Density

How much energy a battery can hold in total.

Power Density

How quickly stored energy can be accessed, especially in bursts.

Self-Discharge Rate

How fast an unused battery loses its charge over time.

Mobile Devices

Smartphones & tablets are designed for "typical" full-day usage without constant charging — and almost all of them run on Li-ion technology.

Battery Chemistry 1 of 3  ·  16 / 30

Nickel-Cadmium (Ni-Cd)

outdated

The laptop-battery standard for decades — now outdated due to environmental concerns, low capacity, and the notorious memory effect. Also heavier than newer chemistries.

The Memory Effect

Repeated partial discharge-and-charge cycles permanently shrink the battery's total capacity over time.

transitional

A more environmentally friendly alternative to Ni-Cd, with little of the memory effect — though it doesn't hold a charge as long as lithium-ion.

winner

The most common battery in laptops today. Lightweight, long-lasting, no memory effect. The clear winner among the three for most modern devices — best balance of weight, lifespan, and performance.

Battery  ·  19 / 30

Battery Accessibility

Laptops
  • Older models: removable, often with a simple switch
  • Recent models: bottom cover removal required
  • Ultra-thin / convertibles: near-complete teardown, wired to motherboard
  • Some have both — diagnose which battery is actually faulty
Mobile Devices
  • Few smartphones/tablets ship with user-removable packs
  • Sealed by design — sleeker body, closed to "adulteration"
  • Replacement may need a heat gun & shims, can void warranty
  • An authorized repair shop is usually the safer call

Why Batteries Fail

Heat — extreme hot or cold

Overcharging / over-discharging

Physical damage — punctures, crushing, bending

Manufacturing defects

Replace, Not Repair

Complex chemistry and real safety risk — especially with Li-ion — make DIY battery repair strongly discouraged.

Internal vs. External

Laptops with internal batteries are "fixed"; laptops with external batteries are not.

External vs. Fixed Batteries

External / Removable
  • Easy to swap if damaged
  • Keep a spare for backup
  • No professional help needed
  • Manufacturer manual covers it
Fixed / Internal
  • Sealed inside the laptop body
  • No backup spare possible
  • Hard to install & replace
  • Professional help recommended

Note: some laptops carry both types — confirm which one is actually at fault before ordering a part.

Battery · Safety  ·  24 / 30

Safety & Environmental Concerns

Before You Open It
  • Disconnect AC power & shut the unit down fully
  • Hold the power button briefly to drain residual charge
Disposal & Storage
  • Contains lithium, mercury, nickel, cadmium — never regular trash
  • Use recycling depots / hazardous-material pickup
  • Never store long-term — casings can corrode, leak, or explode

Battery · Safety  ·  25 / 30

Danger: Swollen Batteries

Li-ion batteries are designed to swell rather than burst or explode — but that doesn't make it safe to ignore.

Common Causes
  • Overcharging
  • Hot, poorly-ventilated environments
Warning Signs
  • Bulging back panel, thicker device
  • Screen protector no longer sits flat
Stop Using It Immediately

A leaking Li-ion battery near an ignition source can catch fire. Ensure good ventilation, keep flammables away, and never leave a battery charging unattended.

Battery Health  ·  26 / 30

Battery Lifespan

Poor battery health usually shows up as a device that barely makes it through the day, or drains fast.

2–3 yrsTypical laptop battery life
3–5 yrsTypical phone battery life
<50%Deep discharges shorten lifespan
<10%Especially damaging if frequent

Both are based on regular daily charging cycles — heavier or deeper discharge cycles age a battery faster than the calendar does.

Battery Health  ·  27 / 30

Charging Issues & Calibration

Improper Charging
  • Faulty AC adapter or wrong wattage
  • Damaged charging cable or port
  • Check the charger's rating matches the device
  • Inspect cables for rips, bends, burn marks
  • Clean charging ports of dust & debris
Calibration & Hard Off
  • A full discharge once a month can fix "digital memory loss" on Li-ion
  • Holding the power button ~5 seconds forces a hard shutdown

Battery Optimization — 12 Tips

Enable power-saving features

Lower screen brightness

Configure a shorter lock timeout

iOS: turn off background app refresh

Avoid draining the battery completely

Partial discharge/recharge extends Li-ion life

Keep devices away from heat & direct sun

Close unneeded apps; restart daily if needed

Disable automatic updates where possible

Turn off unused Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, location

Ensure good airflow — don't cover vents

Power off the device when not in use
